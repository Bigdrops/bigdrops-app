## 2. Research and inspiration categories

Research covered these territories:

- teamwork and handoff motion graphics;
- editorial workplace animation and cutaway composition;
- administrative choreography;
- paper engineering and physical stamp motion;
- workshop and office title sequences;
- top-down work animation;
- coordinated character motion;
- business operations motion identity;
- control-room motion design;
- classic motion principles: anticipation, staging, mass, follow-through, overlapping action.

Principles taken from the research and applied:

| Principle | Application |
| :--- | :--- |
| Anticipation, impact, settle. | The press in "Drafting Table". The drawer in "The Partition". |
| Staging one clear subject per beat. | Every candidate leads the eye with one action at a time. |
| Follow-through and overlapping action. | Trays tick after the document lands. Operator markers settle after a tap. |
| Causal chains beat simultaneous noise. | Every handoff in all six is triggered by the step before it. |
| Springs, not fixed durations, for physical motion. | Curves use overshoot only where a physical object lands. |

## 5. Concept details

### 5.1 Drafting Table — mobile + fold

- **Team metaphor:** the team at one table, seen from directly above.
- **Actors and functions:** hand one steadies the sheet. Hand two collects the client copy. A press approves. Two trays receive.
- **Handoff chain:** sheet arrives, hand steadies it, press approves, a stub tears off to the near tray, the sheet crosses to the records table, the records table sorts and indexes it.
- **First-second event:** the press rises and strikes. The mark lands with a small overshoot at about 27 percent of the first cycle.
- **Ongoing activity:** a second, smaller chain ticks a slip and files it. Tray lamps confirm each landing.
- **Contextual BIGDROPS ideas:** quotation approval, client copy, records filing, indexing.
- **Multitenancy:** not applicable.
- **Tip treatment:** a desk memo clipped to the table edge.
- **Fold transformation:** the table becomes two tables. A records table joins and the filed sheet crosses the join. Fold adds a department.
- **Why it differs:** it is the only overhead view, the only concept with a physical press, and the only one with a two-table handoff.

### 5.2 Many Hands — mobile + fold

- **Team metaphor:** cooperation shown only as hands. No faces and no bodies.
- **Actors and functions:** four hands on phone, six on fold, each with a role label.
- **Handoff chain:** prepare places the sheet, review turns it to reveal the mark, receipt tears the stub, file carries the rest away.
- **First-second event:** two hands snap the sheet into register, and one stub tears off, inside the first second.
- **Ongoing activity:** a small slip crosses the upper field in its own rhythm.
- **Contextual BIGDROPS ideas:** preparation, review, receipt of payment, filing.
- **Multitenancy:** not applicable.
- **Tip treatment:** the tip is handed to the viewer. A note turns to face the camera on a slight perspective rotation.
- **Fold transformation:** the field widens. Two more hands enter from the outer edges and the relay gains two links. Fold adds participants.
- **Why it differs:** it is the only concept with no container, no table, and no grid. The whole screen is negative space and hands.

### 5.3 Two Rooms — mobile + fold

- **Team metaphor:** two businesses working side by side in separate rooms.
- **Actors and functions:** two geometric operator markers, two document streams, two trays.
- **Handoff chain:** within each room only. Intake to process to file.
- **First-second event:** workspace A starts working at once, with its document and operator entering together.
- **Ongoing activity:** workspace B runs at a phase offset of 2.4 seconds, so the two rooms never march in step.
- **Contextual BIGDROPS ideas:** a per-business intake, process, and file cycle. Records stay with their owner.
- **Multitenancy:** this is the first of the two multitenancy concepts. Phone shows one workspace. Fold reveals a second workspace behind a solid wall. A fold-only element shows a record reaching for the wall and returning to its own tray.
- **Tip treatment:** one notice pinned across both rooms. This is the only shared surface.
- **Fold transformation:** a wall appears, and a second workspace becomes visible with its own rhythm. Fold adds an organisation, not width.
- **Why it differs:** it is the only concept built on isolation. Its subject is the boundary, not the flow.

### 5.4 The Ledger — desktop

- **Team metaphor:** the finance team at one long desk.
- **Actors and functions:** entry, classify, tax separation, reconcile, filed. Four function markers confirm the stations.
- **Handoff chain:** an entry enters at the left. A marker confirms it at classify. The tax portion separates and drops to the tax lane. A second marker confirms the reconcile station. The entry files at the right.
- **First-second event:** entries slam into column alignment as they enter.
- **Ongoing activity:** four entries run at once with two seconds between them, so four rows are always at four different stages.
- **Contextual BIGDROPS ideas:** entry, classification, VAT and WHT separation, reconciliation, filing.
- **Multitenancy:** not applicable.
- **Tip treatment:** a ledger footnote with a red bracket, written into the margin.
- **Desktop width behaviour:** five stations span the full width. Entries travel the whole desk. Parallel rows make the width necessary.
- **Safety note:** the screen uses abstract bars and lane blocks. It shows no currency values and no calculated amounts.
- **Why it differs:** it is the only concept with typographic columns and a numeric field. It is the only one where the work is classified rather than carried.

### 5.5 The Switchboard — desktop

- **Team metaphor:** a coordinator assigning work to a team.
- **Actors and functions:** intake, coordinator, five stations, result port.
- **Handoff chain:** intake to coordinator to two or three stations to the result port. The route is traceable end to end.
- **First-second event:** one trigger fires three stations at the same moment.
- **Ongoing activity:** a second routing starts before the first finishes, so routes overlap and occasionally cross.
- **Contextual BIGDROPS ideas:** quotations, invoices, payments, tax, records, and a single filed result.
- **Multitenancy:** not applicable.
- **Tip treatment:** a console directive that slides in, with a priority stamp.
- **Desktop width behaviour:** five stations spread from x = 680 px to x = 1169 px at 1440 px wide. Route lines are long and do not collide.
- **Why it differs:** it is the only dark concept, the only abstract one, and the only one about management rather than production.

### 5.6 The Partition — desktop

- **Team metaphor:** many businesses operating inside one system.
- **Actors and functions:** twelve workspaces, one shared spine, twelve chutes.
- **Handoff chain:** within each workspace only. A document enters, an operator confirms it, and the document settles on that workspace's own stack.
- **First-second event:** the workspaces wake in sequence, 0.43 seconds apart.
- **Ongoing activity:** every workspace runs its own cycle at its own phase. A workspace lights while it works, then goes quiet.
- **Contextual BIGDROPS ideas:** separate record sets, separate filing, one shared system.
- **Multitenancy:** this is the second multitenancy concept. Labels use neutral identifiers only: Workspace 01 to Workspace 12. No company name is invented. Each cell states "own records" and "filed here".
- **Tip treatment:** a label printed on a drawer that pulls out.
- **Desktop width behaviour:** twelve workspaces form a grid across the full width. Three columns appear below 1180 px; four above it.
- **Why it differs:** it is the only grid of compartments, the only concept with more than two parallel organisations, and the only one where the same action repeats in many independent places at once.

## 6. Best concept

**The Ledger** best captures "BIGDROPS as your business team already at work".

Reasons:

1. It shows the largest number of cooperating functions at one time: five stations and four function markers.
2. The handoff is legible in one direction. A viewer can follow one entry from entry to file without instruction.
3. The work has consequences. The tax separation is not decoration. The entry changes state at each station.
4. Several entries work at once at different stages. This reads as a team, not a single process.
5. It keeps the white, slate, and navy character of the retained reference while discarding its gears.
6. The tax lane receives from every row. That shared destination makes the team aspect visible.

**Runner-up: The Switchboard.** It states coordination most directly, because one trigger produces several responses and the results converge into one record. It is the stronger choice if the goal shifts from "work is getting done" to "BIGDROPS is directing the work".

Checks run on the six files:

- Three verbatim rotating tips in each. Confirmed by source and by a runtime check.
- Tip body copy is 15 px on phone and 15 to 16 px on desktop. Confirmed by computed style.
- No emoji. All non-ASCII characters are the em dash, the middle dot, or the arrow.
- No spinner, no loading ring, no progress bar, no percentage, no skeleton, no shimmer, no orbiting element.
- No waybill, truck, route-map, parcel, or delivery concept.
- `prefers-reduced-motion` block in all six files, plus a `matchMedia` guard in every script.
- Console: no errors at load on any of the six.

Defects found during verification and fixed:

1. **The Switchboard** used percentage values in `translate` for pulse travel. A `translate` with a length resolves against the element's own box, so every pulse moved a few pixels instead of across the room. Fixed with viewport units. Confirmed by reading the live transform.
2. **Many Hands** overlapped the tip card with the document at fold width. Fixed by moving the exchange left and deriving the card width from the viewport. Confirmed at 645 px and 760 px.
3. **Drafting Table** lost the mirrored transform on the right hand, because the keyframe transform replaced it. Fixed with a mirror variable inside the keyframe.
4. A CSS comment in **The Switchboard** contained the word "percentages". It was only a comment, but it tripped the loading-cliché scan. Reworded.
5. **The Ledger** staggered the four entries with delays of 0, -2, -4, and -6 seconds, but the animations inside each row had no delay. The bar split, the tax separation, and the confirmation tick therefore fired at the same absolute time for every row. For three rows out of four, the split appeared at the wrong station. This broke the causal rule for the concept. Fixed by letting the row's own delay reach its children. Verification now reads `entryDelays: 0s -2s -4s -6s` and `taxDelays: 0s -2s -4s -6s` together.

Limitations:

- Pixel screenshots could not be captured. The preview webview reported that it was not composited. Visual quality is confirmed by DOM geometry, computed styles, and console output only. Rhythm and feel remain a human judgement.
- The reduced-motion state is verified by source inspection and by the registration of the media rule. There is no tool in this environment to emulate the media feature at runtime.
