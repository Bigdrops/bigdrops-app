## Why This Reference Exists

arc42 provides a taxonomy of fine-grained authorization approaches: RBAC, ABAC, ReBAC. This helps BIGDROPS validate its §3 permission model against established patterns and understand where it sits on the authorization spectrum.

## Relevant Architecture / Patterns

- **RBAC** — Roles map to sets of permissions. Users inherit permissions through roles. BIGDROPS §3 follows this.
- **ABAC** — Attributes (user, resource, environment) determine access. More flexible but more complex.
- **ReBAC** — Relationship-based; access depends on relationships between entities (e.g., "is owner of").
- **Action-resource policies** — policies that specify (action, resource) pairs, not just CRUD verbs.
- **Roles as stable bundles** — roles group related permissions into stable, reusable sets.
- **Attributes for context** — ABAC adds context (time, location, device) to authorization decisions.

## What BIGDROPS Should Borrow

- The **RBAC taxonomy** — validates BIGDROPS's choice of role-based permissions with editable bundles (§3.11).
- The **action-resource** pattern — BIGDROPS abilities like `invoice:create` are action-resource pairs.
- The concept of **roles as stable bundles** — BIGDROPS roles group permissions into reusable sets.
- The warning that **ABAC adds complexity** — BIGDROPS should stay with RBAC unless ABAC is clearly needed.

## What BIGDROPS Must NOT Adopt

- ABAC or ReBAC as the default — BIGDROPS uses RBAC per §3.
- Over-engineering the authorization model — RBAC with editable bundles is sufficient for BIGDROPS's current needs.
- Generic authorization frameworks that assume a PDP/PEP infrastructure BIGDROPS does not have.

## Known Limitations

- Architecture-level guidance; not implementation-specific.
- No entity lifecycle (§8A) coverage.
- Does not address Supabase-specific patterns.

## Bottom Line

Good taxonomy for reasoning about authorization. Validates BIGDROPS's RBAC choice. The action-resource pattern and roles-as-bundles concept are directly applicable to §3.
