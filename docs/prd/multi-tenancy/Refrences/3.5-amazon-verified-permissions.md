## Why This Reference Exists

AWS Verified Permissions demonstrates the per-tenant policy store pattern for multi-tenant authorization. While BIGDROPS does not use AWS AVP, the pattern of tenant-scoped policies and the Cedar policy language provide architectural insight for §3.

## Relevant Architecture / Patterns

- **Per-tenant policy store** — each tenant has its own set of authorization policies. BIGDROPS does not need this at current scale, but the concept is relevant for future enterprise tenants.
- **Cedar policy language** — AWS's policy language for expressing authorization rules. Action-based, not CRUD-based.
- **Action-based permit policies** — policies that permit specific actions on resources. BIGDROPS §3 uses action-based abilities.
- **Tenant boundary + role within tenant** — two-layer authorization: first resolve tenant, then check role within that tenant.

## What BIGDROPS Should Borrow

- The **tenant boundary + role within tenant** concept — BIGDROPS resolves workspace/entity first, then checks permissions within that scope.
- The **action-based permit** pattern — BIGDROPS abilities like `invoice:create` match Cedar's action-based model.
- The concept that **different tenants may need different policy sets** — relevant if BIGDROPS serves enterprise customers with custom permissions.

## What BIGDROPS Must NOT Adopt

- Amazon Verified Permissions as a dependency — BIGDROPS uses its own permission model.
- Cedar policy language — not applicable to BIGDROPS's stack.
- Per-tenant policy stores — over-engineered for BIGDROPS's current needs.
- AWS-specific infrastructure patterns.

## Known Limitations

- AWS-specific; assumes AWS infrastructure.
- Per-tenant policy stores are complex and costly for small-scale SaaS.
- No entity lifecycle (§8A) coverage.
- Cedar is a niche policy language; not widely adopted outside AWS.

## Bottom Line

The tenant-boundary + role-within-tenant concept is directly applicable. The per-tenant policy store pattern is interesting but over-engineered for BIGDROPS's current needs. Use as a conceptual reference only.
